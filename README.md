## The hold: why a $400 reward can lock your whole balance

This is the part almost nobody explains before you sign up.

Invezz's terms summary for OKX's US new-user promotion says certain rewards carry an asset-holding requirement: eligible customers must maintain the specified account balance for a total of 30 days within 90 days of receiving the bonus, and once that's met the reward becomes tradeable and withdrawable. Miss the window and "rewards may be reclaimed."

OKX's own published new-user bonus terms, in the regional versions available for the UAE and EEA programmes, describe the mechanic behind that requirement. When you receive a crypto reward, OKX places a temporary hold on your account balance equal to the live USD market value of the reward. The hold isn't fixed at the value on the day it was paid. It keeps tracking the market price of the original reward quantity, even after you've sold or converted it.

The worked example in OKX's terms is worth walking through, because the outcome is counterintuitive:

- You receive 0.001 BTC as a reward when BTC is at $100,000. The reward is worth $100, so the hold is $100.
- You sell the 0.001 BTC and get $100 USD in your account.
- BTC rises to $120,000. The hold is recalculated as 0.001 BTC × $120,000 = $120.
- Your $100 balance is now smaller than the $120 hold, so your entire balance appears locked, even though that money came from your own sale.

OKX states plainly that this is expected behaviour, not a bug, and that it exists so the reward can be recovered if the holding requirement isn't met. The hold is released when the assets-under-management condition is satisfied and the reward unlocks, or when OKX reclaims the reward.

Two practical consequences. Selling the reward doesn't end your obligation, and a rally in the reward coin after you sell can leave you with more locked than you were ever paid. The terms referenced here are the UAE and EEA versions; the US campaign wording may differ, so read the version shown to your account before depositing.

## Which deposits and trades count, and which quietly don't

OKX's new-user bonus terms exclude a long list of actions that look like qualifying activity but aren't:

- **Internal transfers don't count as deposits.** Moving funds between your own OKX accounts isn't a deposit.
- **Stablecoin pairs and zero-fee pairs don't count as trades.** USDT/USD, USDT/USDC, USDT/USDG, PYUSD/USDT and RLUSD/USDT are named as excluded, along with zero mark-up buy, sell or convert transactions such as USDC/USDT, USDC/USDG, USDC/USD and USDG/USD.

That combination creates an obvious trap. The cheapest way to generate trading volume is usually a stablecoin pair or a Convert order, and those are exactly the routes that don't register toward the milestone. If a milestone needs real volume, you'll be paying maker or taker fees on standard pairs to get it.

Convert deserves its own warning. OKX displays no trading fee on Convert, but the rate is set by OKX, so the cost shows up in the price you get rather than on a fee line.
